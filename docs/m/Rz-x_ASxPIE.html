Discussion

  • Approval of minutes from October 13

    Commissioner Searcy moved to approve the minutes, which were seconded by other commissioners. No errors or omissions were reported. 1:06

  • Contract with Fannin County Chamber of Commerce

    The board discussed a contract for TV services between the county and the chamber. The contract is part of the tourism product development money, which comes to the county every three years. The chamber oversees this money but does not receive any portion of it; instead, funds are used for projects like redoing parks. 2:58

  • Maintenance Department equipment purchase

    Director Shannon Wright presented a proposal to purchase a new truck for the maintenance department. The current Chevrolet truck is nearing the end of its life, and there is another L-250 truck at Dave's Chevrolet that needs to be taken off the road. The new truck will replace both vehicles. 2:58

  • Details on the new truck

    The new truck is a 2026 model with an original price of $62,800. Director Wright negotiated the price down from $65,000 to $62,800. The vehicle has four-wheel drive and more options than the current truck. 9:03

  • Blood cooler purchase

    Director Baking Up discussed purchasing a blood cooler to allow Fannin County to carry its own blood instead of relying on pickups from Yelmer County. The county has a grant covering almost all costs, with the state trauma commission providing $12,000. 10:45

  • Blood cooler specifications and cost

    The blood cooler is portable, compatible with Wi-Fi for tracking blood temperature, and resembles a Yeti cooler. The total cost is approximately $15,000, with the grant covering $12,000 and the county paying the remaining $3,000. 11:59

  • Purchase of blood and plasma units

    Staff explained that they recently administered four units (two red blood cells and two plasma) to a patient in Fannin County after waiting over an hour before meeting with Gilmer, followed by a two-hour transport time. They realized having blood available locally would allow quicker administration for remote areas. The county has an agreement with LifeSouth to pick up blood at Kenniston, replace used units, and return unused blood within two weeks for a refund; plasma cannot be returned. The total cost is $15,889.98, reduced by a $12,000 grant. 13:48

  • Blood supply logistics and costs

    Staff described the process of picking up blood at Kenniston from LifeSouth, replacing used units, and using a delivery service for patients flown out. Unused blood can be returned within two weeks for a refund, but plasma cannot be returned. 14:50

  • Road maintenance and Jasper Road

    A resident discussed Jasper Road in Wolf Creek, noting it was maintained by the county from 1994-95 to 2017/18 (approximately 23 years). Although there were no formal records of its inclusion on the road list, the length of maintenance suggests it became a public road by prescription. The speaker referenced Georgia Code regarding road abandonment and the requirement for a determination of public interest. 17:12

  • Legal requirements for road abandonment

    A board member stated that a road cannot be abandoned simply to benefit a private landowner and requires a formal determination that it serves no substantial public purpose.
