Summary

The City Council Workshop reviewed the Fiscal Year 2027 proposed budget presented by Eric Soroka, including general fund increases and utility extension policies supported by Commissioner Hensley. The session also addressed open records, a winter ice skating event contract, public comment on homelessness, and suggestions for long-term harm-reduction strategies.

Discussion

  • Fiscal Year 2027 Proposed Budget Overview

    Eric Soroka presented the FY 2027 budget totaling $20.2 million, representing a $3 million (18%) increase over the prior year. 0:49

  • Budget Increase Breakdown

    Capital expenditures accounted for almost $2 million (63% of the increase), operating expenditures totaled $10.4 million (9.8% increase), and debt service was $1.3 million (21% increase). 1:06

  • Capital Costs Increase

    Capital costs increased by 30% or $1.9 million to fund additional projects and address infrastructure needs. 1:18

  • General Fund Budget

    The general fund budget is approximately $5 million ($4.9 million), with revenues projected to increase by 4%. 1:35

  • Tax Impact

    The rollback rate was adopted to avoid a tax increase, but total taxes still represent an increase of $126,000 over the prior year. 2:02

  • General Fund Revenue Sources

    Sales tax receipts make up the largest revenue source at $1.5 million (31% of the budget). Without prior year fund balance, revenues would only increase by $27,000. 2:15

  • General Fund Highlights

    Highlights include no new employee positions, funding for records management software upgrades/scanning, two elections, and a $50,000 contingency fund. 2:38

  • Departmental Budget Changes

    The finance department was split from general administration into its own budget. Public Works has a replacement truck and $250,000 for sidewalks/safety improvements. Parks has a replacement truck, dump addition, and five movie nights. 2:49

  • Hotel Motel Fund

    The FY 2027 hotel motel fund is $2.4 million, including projects like train depot repairs, farmers market painting, and downtown bathrooms. 3:23

  • SPLOST Fund

    The FY 2027 SPLOST fund is about $1.7 million, covering roads, drainage, sidewalks ($650k), and capital projects in the Water and Sewer Fund. 4:01

  • Water and Sewer Fund

    The Water and Sewer Fund is budgeted at $1.1 million, a $2 million (22%) increase driven by $1.5 million in additional capital projects. 4:21

  • Water and Sewer Rate Increases

    To support increased operating costs and infrastructure improvements, water rates are recommended to increase by 4% and sewer rates by 4.5%. 4:47

  • Water and Sewer Fund Projects

    Over 53% of the fund addresses rehabilitation of sewer lift stations, replacing aging water lines/mains, upgrading pump stations, and addressing water loss. 5:12

  • Debt Service Fund

    A new Debt Service Fund was established to address debt service for the G-FOOT downtown stormwater loan fund, budgeted at $103,000 funded via transfer from the general fund. 5:48

  • Executive Position Funding

    The $215,000 for the Assistant Mayor position is funded out of the executive portion; the Field Maintenance Operation Superintendent costs are split 60% from one fund and 40% from the Water and Sewer fund. 7:09

  • Contracted Services Increase

    There is about a half million increase in contracted services across all funds, including Public Works, Parks, General Administration, Finance, Stormwater, and Engineering. 8:05

  • Property Tax Increase

    There is no increase in property taxes; the total remains at $127,000. 8:54

  • General Fund Budget

    Staff noted capital is being drawn from the general fund and suggested shifting some spending to future years via SPLOs to free up funds. The council questioned the 4% budget increase assumption on a $4 million budget, noting it includes sales tax ($800,000) and alcohol tax revenues compared to a conservative prior year. 9:00

  • Water/Sewer Fund Rates

    Staff explained that water/sewer rate increases require a separate ordinance vote in January or February. If passed, the increase would amount to about $200,000; if not, cuts would be needed. 10:43

  • Hotel-Motel Fund Estimates

    Staff confirmed the hotel-motel fund list is an estimate that can be changed via amendment but cannot exceed the $2.4 million total allocation without a council vote. 11:48

  • Utility Extension and Service Policy

    Staff reported that Commissioner Hensley is agreeable to formalizing the utility extension policy through an intergovernmental agreement. A council member supported this, stating it gives Blue Ridge a seat at the table regarding development decisions outside city limits. 15:12

  • Comprehensive Pay Plan

    Staff presented the updated comprehensive pay plan, developed using GMA comparisons and data from similarly sized cities. Staff confirmed salary data is subject to open records requests. 18:25

  • Open records availability

    Staff confirmed that while current year data is not yet posted, last year's data is available online via opengeorgia.gov. Future fiscal year data will be posted to the DCA website after the fiscal year closes. 19:47

  • Blue Ridge on Ice contract review

    Staff reviewed the proposed contract for a winter ice skating event at the farmers market, noting it is similar to prior years and plans to begin operations around Thanksgiving. The contract will be presented for approval at next week's meeting. 20:36

  • 2027 council meetings schedule

    Staff presented proposed dates for 2027 council workshops and planning commission meetings. A question was raised regarding potential conflicts with the November 2nd election; staff confirmed no conflicts exist as the only Thursday meeting is scheduled for November 4th. 22:16

  • Criticism of current homeless management approach

    The speaker argued that the current method is impractical and causes more problems than it solves, citing personal inability to walk long distances for work. 29:14

  • Proposed cooperative and harm-reduction strategy

    The speaker offered to help establish out-of-sight locations around town to monitor residents and ensure they behave as good neighbors. 29:53
