Upgrade 1: better summaries with Claude

Summaries are written today by a small AI model (qwen3.5, 4 billion parameters) that runs on the same computer as everything else. Scored against the official minutes, it finds most votes but misses some, and it needs a lot of checking code around it. The plan is to switch to Claude Sonnet 5.5, a much stronger model made by Anthropic, through its paid API.

How it would work: the summarizer already supports Claude; switching is one setting. Everything else stays the same: code still finds the votes, checks every quote and count against the transcript, and scores each version against the official minutes before it's used on the site. Transcription and speaker matching stay on the local computer, so only text is sent to the API.

What it costs: $2 per million word pieces ("tokens") read and $10 per million written (Anthropic's price list). A 45-minute meeting is about 45,000 tokens in and 8,000 out, about $0.17. Budgeting $0.30 a meeting for longer ones, and about 80 meetings a year across the four boards, comes to $24 a year.

The local model used about 30,000 input and 5,000 output tokens on a 45-minute meeting; the estimate adds 50% for longer meetings, speaker naming, and Claude's different tokenizer. Meetings per year: about 30 City Council, 10 Planning Commission, 24 Board of Commissioners and 12 Board of Education.

Upgrade 2: email alerts by topic

Pick topics, like zoning, the budget, a board, or a road, and get an email when a meeting covers one. This was planned as a free feature and was dropped: doing it for free would mean storing people's email addresses on this project's own systems, and running the signup, confirmation and unsubscribe pieces that protect them. A newsletter service does that job better, but not for free.

How it would work: a signup box on this site, run by Buttondown, where you choose your topics. Buttondown keeps the list, the confirmations and the unsubscribe links; this project never stores email addresses. After each new summary, the pipeline sends one email to the people whose topics came up.

What it costs: Buttondown Basic, $9 a month or $90 a year (price list).

Buttondown's free plan holds up to 100 subscribers, but sending through its API (needed for automatic alerts) and subscriber tags (needed for topics) are on paid plans. MailerLite's free plan doesn't include sending through its API either.
